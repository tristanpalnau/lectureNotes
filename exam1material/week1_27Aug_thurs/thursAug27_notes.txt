All ints.

if (x < 3 || y > 4) {
    statements1;
}
else if (x < 5 && y == 7) {
    statements2;
}
else {
    statements3;
}

--> is it possible for "statements2" to execute?
first if condition has to be false
    x < 3 || y > 4 is false
    x < 3 is false AND y > 4 is false
    x >= 3 AND y <= 4

the (first) else if condition has to be true
    x < 5 && y == 7 is true
    x < 5, also y == 7

putting them together: x would have to be 4
    y would have to be both <= 4 AND == 7, not possible

since y's values aren't possible, statements2 can never execute

More with knights and knaves.

knights: each full statement that they make must be true
knave: each full statement must be false


You see two different inhabitants, Ava and Bob.

Ava says that Bob is a knave.
Bob says, "Neither Ava nor I are knaves."


What does "neither" mean? both false

Rewrite Bob: Ava is not a knave and I am not a knave


    Ava says that Bob is a knave.
    Bob: Ava is a knight and I am a knight


What are Ava and Bob? 
    Ava: knight, Bob: knave

Suppose Ava is a knight. Then from her statement, Bob would have
    to be a knave. Then Bob's statement is correctly false -- they
    are not both knights. 

Suppose instead that Ava is a knave. From her statement, Bob
    would have to be a knight. Bob's statement would be a lie
    because they are not both knights, which doesn't work.

You meet three inhabitants: Sarah, Bill and Mae.

Sarah tells you that only a knave would say that Bill is a knave.
Bill claims that it's false that Mae is a knave.
Mae tells you, "Bill would tell you that I am a knave."

What types are Sarah, Bill, and Mae?


(How might we simplify everything?)
    Sarah: Bill is a knight 
    Bill: Mae is a knight 
    Mae: Bill would tell you that I am a knave.

Suppose Sarah is a knight. From her statement, Bill must be a knight,
    and from his statement, Mae must be a knight. Mae's statement
    doesn't work -- Bill (a knight) would not say Mae is a knight.

Suppose instead Sarah is a knave. From her statement and then Bill's 
    statement, Bill and Mae would both be knaves. Looking at Mae's 
    statement, Bill would lie and say she is a knight. But Mae would 
    lie about what Bill say, and would say Bill would say knave.

Lion always lies on Mondays, Tuesdays, and Wednesdays.
Lion always tells the truth on other days.
Unicorn always lies on Thursdays, Fridays, and Saturdays,
    and always tells the truth on other days.

On Sunday, everyone tells the truth.

Lion says: "Yesterday was one of my lying days."
Unicorn says: "Yesterday was one of my lying days, too."

What day is it? only 7 possible days 
    Sunday? no. They didn't both lie on Saturday, and on Sunday
        they should both tell the truth.
    Tuesday? Lion should lie, Unicorn should tell truth. 
        Yesterday WAS one of Lion's lying days, and Lion SHOULD lie
        about that. Doesn't work. 
    Fridays/Wednesdays/Saturdays? Similar to Tuesday
    Monday? Lion should lie, Unicorn should tell truth. Doesn't 
        work for Unicorn - Unicorn told truth on Sunday.
    Thursday? Unicorn lies, Lion tells truth. Works out -
        Unicorn lies that Wed was a lying day, Lion tells truth 
        that Wed was a lying day.

Day: Thursday

Based on Shakespeare's "Merchant of Venice".

There are three caskets – one gold, one silver,
    and one lead.
Exactly one of the caskets contains a portrait.
Each casket has a message on it, and you know that at most
    one of the messages is true.

(could be one message true, could be 0 messages true)

Gold casket: "The portrait is in this casket."
Silver casket: "The portrait is not in this casket."
Lead casket: "The portrait is not in the gold casket."

Where is the portrait? (only three possibilities)

Suppose it is in the gold casket. 
    gold: T, silver: T (not possible - more than 1 is true)

Suppose it is in the silver casket.
    gold: F, silver: F, (works - at most 1 would be true)

Suppose it is in the lead casket.
    gold: F, silver: T, lead: T (not possible - more than 1 is true)



Answer: silver

Cheryl's birthday problem.

Written for Singapore and Asian Schools Math Olympiad in 2015

Albert and Bernard want to know when Cheryl's birthday is.
Cheryl gives them 10 potential dates:

May 15, May 16, May 19
June 17, June 18
July 14, July 16
August 14, August 15, August 17

Cheryl privately tells Albert her birthday month.
    (May, June, July, or August)

Cheryl privately tells Bernard her birthday day (number).
    (14-19)




Albert says: "I don't know when Cheryl's birthday is,
    but I know Bernard doesn't know too."

must be a month with no unique days (can't be May or June)


Bernard says: "At first I didn't know when Cheryl's
    birthday is, but now I do."

(now Bernard also knows it's not May or June)   
can't be a non-repeating day or Bernard wouldn't be sure
can't be July 14 or Aug 14


Albert says: "Then I also know when Cheryl's birthday is."
can't be August - there are still two days remaining
must be July 16


When is it? July 16
