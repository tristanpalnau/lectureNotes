Suppose we have these propositional atoms:

    p: It is below freezing.
    q: It is snowing.
    r: y <= 4
    s: x == 2

How would we express these as English sentences/conditions?

    ¬p: It is not below freezing 
    p ∧ q: It is below freezing and it is snowing
    p → q : if it is below freezing, then it is snowing
    ¬p → ¬q: if it's not below freezing, then it is not snowing
    r ∨ s: y <= 4 || x == 2
    ¬r: y > 4

Example:

You can't enroll for the course if you haven't taken 
the prerequisite or the course is full.

Really saying: if you haven't taken the prerequisite or
    the course is full, then you can't enroll for the course

--> what are the propositional atoms?

p: you can enroll for the course
q: you have taken the prerequisite
r: the course is full

--> translate to propositional logic:
¬q ∨ r → ¬p


What is the *contrapositive* of our statement?

p → q has a contrapositive of ¬q → ¬p


¬q ∨ r → ¬p
has contrapositive of: p → ¬(¬q ∨ r)
    equivalent after DeMorgan: p → q ∧ ¬r

How do we prove two statements ARE equivalent?

complete two truth tables

have the same output

How do we prove two statements are NOT equivalent?
