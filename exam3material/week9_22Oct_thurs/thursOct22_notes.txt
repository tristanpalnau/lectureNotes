Revisit our example:

We want to prove that for any positive integer n:

    1 + 2 + ... + n = n*(n+1)/2


The above claim is P(n)


Proof by induction.

Base case. We must prove the claim holds for the first positive integer n,
n = 1. We have that the left side of the claim is 1, and the right side
of the claim is: 1(1+1)/2 = 1. The two sides are equal, so the base case holds.

Inductive step. We assume the inductive hypothesis - that 
    1 + 2 + ... + k = k(k+1)/2
for some fixed positive integer k. We must show that our claim holds
for k+1. That is, we must show that:
    1 + 2 + ... + k + (k+1) = (k+1)((k+1)+1)/2

We have that:
    1 + 2 + ... + k + (k+1) = k(k+1)/2 + (k+1) (by inductive hypothesis)
                            = (k(k+1) + 2(k+1))/2
                            = (k+1)(k+2)/2
                            = (k+1)((k+1)+1)/2

Thus the claim holds for k+1.

We have proved the base case and the inductive step, so our
claim holds by induction for all positive integers n.

Example: Prove that 3^n - 1 is even for all positive integers n.


what does "even" mean? divisible by 2
    {x : x = 2k, k in Z, k >= 1}



Proof by induction.

Base case. We must prove that our claim holds for the smallest positive
integer, n = 1. We have that 3^1 - 1 = 2. Since 2 = 2*1 where 1 is an
integer, we have that 2 is even. The base case holds.

Inductive step. We assume the inductive hypothesis -- that 3^k - 1
is even for some fixed positive integer k. We must prove that
3^(k+1) - 1 is also even.

Since 3^k-1 is even, we have 3^k - 1 = 2a where a is an integer.
We have that:
    3^(k+1) - 1 = 3*3^k - 1
                = 3*(3^k - 1) - 1 + 3
                = 3*(3^k - 1) + 2
                = 3*2a + 2
                = 6a + 2
                = 2(3a + 1)

And so 3^(k+1) - 1 = 2(3a+1), where 3a + 1 is an integer. Thus
3^(k+1) - 1 is even.
