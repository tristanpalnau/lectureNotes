Prove that the statements:

    p ∧ q → r

and

    (p → r) ∧ (q → r)

are NOT logically equivalent.

Afterward, explain why you have shown that the two statements are not equivalent.
What would you expect to happen differently if both statements were equivalent?


Consider the truth assignment:
    p = T
    q = F
    r = F

For p ∧ q → r, that truth assignment would give us:
    T ∧ F → F,
    which is F → F,
    which is true.


For (p → r) ∧ (q → r), that truth assignment would give us:
    (T → F) ∧ (F → F)
    which is F ∧ T,
    which is false.


The two statements have different outputs on that truth assignment,
which tells us that they are not logically equivalent. If they were equivalent, we would
expect for them to have the output for every truth assignment.
