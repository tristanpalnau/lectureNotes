
Use mathematical induction to prove that if n is natural number, then:

    1*1! + 2*2! + ... + n*n! = (n+1)! - 1



Base case: We must prove our claim for the smallest positive integer, n = 1.
    The left side of our claim is 1*1! = 1.
    The right side of our claim is (1+1)! - 1 = 2 - 1 = 1.
The two sides are equal, so the base case holds.


Inductive step: We assume the inductive hypothesis -- that 1*1! + 2*2! + ... + k*k! = (k+1)! - 1
for some natural number k. We must show that our claim holds for k+1, so we need to prove that:
    1*1! + 2*2! + ... + k*k! + (k+1)(k+1)! = ((k+1)+1)! - 1

We have that:

    1*1! + 2*2! + ... + k*k! + (k+1)(k+1)! = (k+1)! - 1 + (k+1)(k+1)! (by inductive hypothesis)
                                           = (k+1)!(1 + (k+1)) - 1
                                           = (k+2)(k+1)! - 1
                                           = (k+2)! - 1
                                            = ((k+1) + 1)! - 1

Thus our claim holds for k+1, and so the inductive step holds.


We have shown that for all positive integers n, 1*1! + 2*2! + ... + n*n! = (n+1)! - 1.
