On the island of Knights and Knaves, every inhabitant is either a knight or a knave.
Knights always tell the truth, and knaves always lie.

5) You see inhabitants Gabe, Harry, and Caitlin.

Gabe says: All three of us are knaves.
Caitlin says: Exactly one of us is a knight.
(Harry doesn't say anything.)

//type your solution (knight or knave) next to each question
What type is Gabe? knave
What type is Harry? knave
What type is Caitlin? knight

//Explain your reasoning below. Why is your answer the ONLY one that works?

Suppose Gabe is a knight. Then his statement doesn't work, as not all three of them would be knaves.
Suppose instead that Gabe is a knave. Then at least one between Caitlin and Harry must be a knight (to make Gabe's statement false).
   Suppose that Caitlin is a knight. For her statement to be true, Harry would need to be a knave. This would also correctly make Gabe's
       statement false, as not all of them would be knaves.
   Suppose instead that Caitlin is a knave. Then it couldn't be the case that Harry was a knight, because then her statement would be true.
       It also couldn't be the case that Harry was a knave, since this would make Gabe's statement true instead of false.

6) You see inhabitants Gabby, Lewis, and Ellie.

Gabby says: Lewis and I are both knights.
Lewis says: Ellie would say I am a knight.
Ellie says: Either I am a knight or Lewis is a knight, but not both of us.

//type your solution (knight or knave) next to each question
What type is Gabby? knave
What type is Lewis? knave
What type is Ellie? knight

//Explain your reasoning below. Why is your answer the ONLY one that works?

Suppose Gabby is a knight. Then Lewis must also be a knight.
   Looking at Lewis's statement, Ellie must be a knight (only a knight would truthfully say Lewis is a knight).
   Ellie's statement wouldn't work in this configuration, since both she and Lewis would be knights (which makes her statement false).

Suppose instead that Gabby is a knave. From Gabby's statement, Marshall could be either a knight or a knave (either would make her statement false).
   Suppose Lewis is a knight. This causes the same problem as before -- Ellie would need to be a knight, but that would make her statement false.
   Suppose Lewis is a knave. This means Ellie would actually say that he was a knave.
       Suppose Ellie is a knight. This makes Lewis's statement work, as Ellie would truthfully say that Lewis was a knave.
           Ellie's statement works too, as only one of her and Lewis is a knight.
       Suppose instead that Ellie is a knave. Lewis's statement wouldn't work, as a knave wouldn't say that Lewis was a knave (because that would be true).

7) Your award-winning koi fish was stolen from your pond. You know the thief was either Agatha, Ferdinand, or Carl.

       Agatha says: "Don't look at me! I didn't steal your oversized goldfish."
       Carl says: "Me either! I prefer turtles anyway."

       Then you ask Ferdinand, "Did Agatha and Carl tell the truth?"
       Ferdinand replies, "At least one of them did."

   You later learn that Carl and Ferdinand were not both telling the truth. (Careful -- this is a different statement
   than "Carl and Ferdinand were both not telling the truth".)


   --> Who stole your koi? Explain your answer.

   It was Carl!

   Suppose Agatha stole the koi. Then we can evaluate the truth of each person's statement as follows:
       Agatha: false
       Carl: true
       Ferdinand: true

   However, we know that Carl and Ferdinand were not both telling the truth, so this is not possible.


   Suppose instead that Carl stole the koi. Then we can evaluate the truth of each person's statement as follows:
       Agatha: true
       Carl: false
       Ferdinand: true

   Furthermore, it IS the case that Carl and Ferdinand were not both telling the truth, which works.


   Finally, suppose that Ferdinand stole the koi. We again evaluate the truth of each animal's statement:
       Agatha: true
       Carl: true
       Ferdinand: true

   However, this has both Carl and Ferdinand telling the truth, which we were told did not happen.

   Conclusion: Carl stole the koi
